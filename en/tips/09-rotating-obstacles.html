Rotating a piece over a bump

Rotating the J over a bump, which turns Mihara’s conspiracy to your advantage.
A well-known way of getting the I piece over an obstacle: rotate it flat, then upright again.
It can also be combined with synchro.

Rotating three times to get across a gap

A technique in which you rotate twice to turn the three-column-wide edge downward and get across a two-column-wide gap.
It takes three rotations in all, because you rotate once more after getting across.
The key is whether you can do the first two rotations quickly.
It gets easier once you acquire the feel of deliberately charging DAS late.
Getting across to the left leaves you less room than getting across to the right.
It is fine when the piece drops down a step as you first move it sideways or as it gets across, but when there is no step the timing becomes extremely tight.
One way of dealing with this is to flick right and then charge left the instant the piece appears, doing the second rotation in between.
