Hops

A J “hop” that turns the “conspiracy” to your advantage.
The I piece hop commonly known as the “flip-flap” (パタパタ, pata-pata).
It can also be combined with synchro.

The triple-rotation crossing

A technique in which you rotate twice to turn the three-column-wide edge downward and get across a two-column-wide gap.
It is called the “triple-rotation crossing” because you rotate once more after getting across.
The key is whether you can do the first two rotations quickly.
It gets easier once you acquire the feel of deliberately charging DAS late.
Crossing to the left leaves you less room than crossing to the right.
It is fine when the piece drops down a step as you first move it sideways or as it gets across, but when there is no step the timing becomes extremely tight.
One way of dealing with this is a technique called the “feint”: the instant the piece appears, you quickly enter “right, charge left” on the stick and do the second rotation in between.
