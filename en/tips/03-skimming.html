Skimming (clearing from the middle, mid-air clears)

Also known as “clearing from the middle”, a “mid-air clear” and so on, this is a placement that at first glance seems to create a hole, but which leaves a clean shape behind once the line clears.

Skims for getting by without ruining the well you are keeping for the I.
These are much the same, but notice that they let you lengthen the one-wide part.

All told, you can skim with five kinds of piece, everything but the Z, so keeping the two rightmost columns on the low side is a big advantage.

Skimming to make a flat stack

Skims for making a flat surface.
Lay an I flat and you can sometimes make a line in a place you would not expect. This is handy in TAP’s advanced stages and the like for getting rid of an I that would be in the way if you stood it upright.
Skims that exploit a piece’s long reach.
They also have the effect of lengthening a two-wide gap.
You can also drop a J in like this and clear a double.
If the wait is the other way around, an L does the same job.
It is also handy to remember that the spot flips between waiting for a Z and waiting for an S across the clear.
