From level 500 on, ARE and the line clear delay are marginally shorter than in the 400s. Even so, if you can stack the 400s solidly, it is not a speed you cannot handle.
The problems are that it is a long haul of five sections, and that you have to fight various illusions that make the game feel faster even though the speed has not changed.

The lock delay reset trap

Lock delay reset does not happen only when you are deliberately buying time.
In situations where late charges are bound to happen again and again, “the time the piece stayed in the center + the lock delay after it drops” becomes the “apparent lock delay”.
In particular, a situation that comes up often is one where “if you push the stick after the piece appears, a drop (= a lock delay reset) takes place at that point”.
In this kind of “droppable situation”, as long as you start charging sideways at any moment you like before the piece locks, you will always make it.
Whether a piece is “droppable” also varies with the type of piece and with whether you rotate it, so as for whether you can keep track of it piece by piece during actual play (while interfering as little as possible with your thinking about stacking), that takes a great deal of getting used to.
Usually, I suspect, we mostly play without knowing whether the piece is “droppable”, pay no particular attention to late charges either, and stack by relying on the rhythm at which pieces are actually appearing.
But what happens when, after this slowed-down state of affairs has gone on for a while, you are suddenly faced with an “undroppable situation”? Does it not feel as though the game has abruptly sped up?

The shape you are waiting with

Once you are reasonably experienced, it seems as though you decide where a piece goes in an instant, but even so there are cases where “a placement is found relatively easily” and cases where it is not.
If a run of situations continues in which you find the placement without much thought, the brain gets used to “not thinking much”.
So what happens when you then get a difficult shape for which no suitable placement is easy to find, or conversely a situation in which any number of reasonable placements present themselves?

Once your personal best gets into the 700s or 800s, it happens that you were stacking so cleanly and with so much room to spare that you felt you could not possibly die, and then suddenly you gogogo and are dead on the spot.
When the situation gets faster and harsher, the brain tries to get used to that and thinks faster; but conversely, as it gets slower and more lenient, the brain gets used to that too and stops thinking much.
Bear in mind that stacking cleanly goes hand in hand with the danger of a gogogo. It is important not to let your brain get lazy: do not stop thinking as soon as you have come up with one placement, but produce several candidates, for instance.

Distance to NEXT

You may “read NEXT by color out of the corner of your eye”, but all the same, if it is far away (that is, if the stack is low), you must surely have to spend slightly more mental effort to take it in.
Normally you are not conscious of any such thing. But what about when you have brought the stack down all at once with a string of line clears or the like?
From a situation in which you had been looking squarely at the stack and NEXT at the same time, you go to one in which you have to take in NEXT from the corner of your eye.
Your judgment really does become marginally slower. Yet as far as the player is concerned, there is no awareness of that. And so the game feels faster.

We tend to think that “making the stack low and flat” puts death further off and is safe, but when gravity is high it is actually dangerous; keep that in mind.
Conversely, even when the stack has got high because you gogogo’d or the like, “as long as the top two rows are clear, you will not die”, and if you tell yourself that NEXT is now closer and easier to see, you should be able to play with more composure.
