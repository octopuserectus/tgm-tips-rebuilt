Basic twists

With BPS-style rotation there are cases where the piece won’t turn or pops out upward, but under the Sega-style rotation system, where a piece rotates so that the height of its lowest edge stays the same, twists are very easy to use.
Thanks to the wall kick introduced in Chapter 1, the piece turns with either rotation direction.
The J and the L go in too.
Be careful with these: depending on which way the piece is facing, only one rotation direction will work.
A well-known twist that kicks off the wall.
It takes two rotations but is not all that hard.
You don’t have to hold the stick to the left; leaving it at neutral and rotating is fine.
Use it as much as you can from early on, and get over the fear that the piece might slip down.
The S and the J go to the left easily, and the Z and the L to the right, so this is very easy to use and effective.

Twists that make use of the wall kick

Thanks to the wall kick, you can rotate quickly while keeping the stick charged to the right.
This neatly tidies up an awkward shape that you would normally have to deal with by putting an O in first.
Note that here the piece will not rotate unless you first push it all the way in.
A twist in which the piece tumbles over.
It lets a piece pass through a one-column-wide gap, and comes in useful for things like covering for a misplaced I piece.

Mihara’s conspiracy

→
When a rotated piece overlaps the stack and there is room to shift either left or right, the game is set up so that the piece shifts to the right. This is called “Mihara’s conspiracy.”
The name originally comes from the situation in the left figure: you wanted to tuck the J over to the left, yet it goes to the right (even if you are holding the stick left), which led people to say, “It’s a conspiracy by the developers!”
A twist that lays the piece face down.
Recognizing the spots where it fits takes a little getting used to, but thanks to the conspiracy described above you can rotate with the stick still held to the left.
It is quick, and what’s more it leaves a clean shape afterward. One for time attack.
Laying the J face down is possible if there is stack in a position that stops the piece from shifting right.

When a wall kick is not possible

• The O, of course, and also the I piece do not wall kick.
• With IRS the piece rotates and then appears, so no wall kick takes place.
• The kick is sideways only. A piece cannot kick off the floor or the ceiling.
• The T, J and L cannot wall kick when only the center column overlaps the stack. (For example, a J like the one in the figure is in reality stuck and cannot be moved.)
