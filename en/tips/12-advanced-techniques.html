Special twists

A twist that tucks a J or an L in under an overhang. When doing it with an L, watch out for Mihara’s conspiracy.
A more unusual twist. Unless you have misplaced a J or an L, you will not get much chance to use it.
With a Z, “charge left, C, B, A” does it. With an S, watch out for the conspiracy.
Various I-piece twists.
Use two buttons and you can rotate in an instant.

Getting across with a synchro

Getting a piece across with a synchro.
This uses the synchro introduced in Chapter 7 to move the piece one column while it is in mid-air.
There are two ways to do it: move the piece with DAS and press the button by timing the moment it reaches the right spot (timing it by eye), or take it there with a double tap (two separate taps of the stick) and then input the stick and the rotation simultaneously from there.
Getting an S across with a synchro.
The piece is lifted into the air with a wall kick and then moved sideways.
You can also get pieces such as the T and L across with a synchro.
You will hardly ever get a chance to use them. If anything, the thing to watch out for is that, once you have pared your time-attack inputs down to the limit, you can set one off by accident when all you meant to do was flip the piece normally.

Other techniques

Standing a piece upright with a synchro, done with a simultaneous input.
With it you can climb a step without charging.
Rotating three times in quick succession.
By using A and C together, you can get the second rotation in quickly while the piece is moving sideways, without charging late.
Besides rotating three times to get across a two-wide gap, it can also be used when placing an L on the right as in the figure. Here you have to return to neutral for the last rotation, so doing it quickly takes a good deal of practice.
Combinations of three quick rotations, standing the piece upright with a synchro, and the slide.
I have already said that a sideways input and a rotation can be done simultaneously within one frame; a right rotation and a left rotation can also be done simultaneously within one frame.
Rotation is always carried out before falling, so it is pointless unless the situation is one where the first rotation can wall kick.
In the figure on the left, inputting “IRS-C, right, B+A+down” lets you lock in the same time as one tap sideways where a double tap would normally be needed.
