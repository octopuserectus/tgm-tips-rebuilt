The level 500 wall

• It starts at about the speed of the level 200s in TAP Death mode.
• The level 300s and 400s are about as fast as level 500 in Death mode.
• You must pass level 500 within 2:28.

Think of the early game as a training ground for looking at three next pieces, and aim for 30 seconds per section. Rather than trying to lock every single piece as fast as possible, you may end up faster overall by concentrating on manual locking in a steady rhythm.

As for manual locking from level 300 on, it is possible to pass level 500 without it, as in the S10 movie (tgm3shirase.mpg) published by Arika, but with what comes later in mind I think you’re better off being able to do it. As I wrote in the previous chapter, though, you don’t want to get carried away with speed and have everything collapse at once, so until you’re used to it don’t keep it going for long: break it into bursts of a few pieces, checking as you go whether the stack is OK and whether you’re still looking at three next pieces.

Level 500 to level 1000

• At level 500 the lock delay and line clear delay get shorter.
• At level 600 the lock delay gets shorter.
• Garbage rises from below, each row a copy of the bottom row of your stack. The details aren’t known, but typically it comes up 2 rows per section in the 500s and 600s, 4 rows per section in the 700s and 800s, and 5–6 rows in the 900s.

At first a single rising row may feel as if the stack has suddenly become enormously high, but don’t give in to that pressure. Excessive fear is enough all by itself to sap your ability to think. I think it’s fine to take the defiant view that if the rising garbage crushes you, that’s an accident and there was nothing to be done about it. If you keep the stack waiting for an I piece at the right edge, all that happens is your Tetris well gets one row deeper. Even from 700 on, when the garbage comes faster, what rises is no more than a single Tetris will wipe out. Think of it this way: you “go for Tetrises” not because you have room to spare, but because stacking that way is safer. When the stack gets low, or a piece comes that’s a bit awkward to place, don’t hesitate to put a lid over the well at the right edge and close it off. While you’re busy recovering from that lid, the Tetris well will rise back up all by itself. Once you start to see the rising garbage as “help” rather than a “hindrance,” you’ve got it made.

Because the lock delay is short, the effective lock delay swings wildly depending on step resets, and there’s a high risk that just when you think you’re stacking with some room to spare a piece suddenly locks on you before you know it. Carry your DAS charge over from piece to piece and learn “the fastest rhythm (without manual locking).” At first you beat out the rhythm in your head from the piece spawn sound and from what you see, but once you’re able to learn the speed through “the movement of your hands”—the rhythm you need to operate at—your head is free to concentrate on thinking about the stack. With stacking, too, doing the same thing all the time tends to make your thinking sluggish. After carrying the DAS charge over for a while, tidy the stack by dropping pieces down steps while you look over the whole stack; build up fairly high, then dig back down low; tell yourself “I skimmed just now, so this time let’s try building up without clearing lines”—I think it’s good to give your stacking some light and shade like this.

Level 1000 and beyond

• The garbage stops rising.
• Pieces made up of white [ ] blocks start falling.
• At level 1100 the lock delay gets shorter.
• At level 1200 the lock delay gets shorter.

Lock delay and the like don’t change around level 1000, but because everything is harder to see and your inputs tend to lag, it may feel as if the game has slowed down. First you have to get used to that poor visibility. With the stack, sometimes “a rough idea is enough,” and you can also “memorize” it, but with NEXT, in almost every case it’s worthless unless you can tell which kind of piece it is. The height and bumpiness of the stack change constantly, so there’s no fixed point where you should be looking; I think it’s something you pick up by feel, moving your gaze up and down over and over until you acquire a “division of attention” that lets you keep track of both NEXT and the stack.

At level 1100 the speed has gone up quite a bit, so you may find yourself clearing lines frequently in an effort to get the stack lower and lower, but that means “the stack changes” more violently. Keeping track of the stack gets harder, and if your gaze gradually drifts away from the three next pieces, that leads straight to death. As I said further up, you want “stacking with light and shade.” Once you’re reasonably used to it, prepare for the level 1200s by carrying the DAS charge over a lot and generating as much “speed” as you can. You’ll be plunging into the same speed either way, but it should be easier to cope with if the change is a smaller one rather than an abrupt one.

In the level 1200s, too, “learning the speed through the movement of your hands,” as described above, gives you an advantage, but if you just play normally that speed is itself something you’ve never experienced, so it’s hard to acquire from experience of the 1200s alone. That’s where I think it’s a good idea to take on manual locking after level 500. This lets you generate a rhythm in the same class as the level 1200s (or even faster). ARE doesn’t change after level 300, so if you can manual-lock with reasonably tight timing, you should be able to do it in that same rhythm from 500 on as well. At this stage the purpose of manual locking is purely to “drill the rhythm into your hands,” not time attack, so don’t force yourself to keep it going: the moment the stack looks even slightly dangerous, break off and devote yourself to leveling it out. Once you know the rhythm and can see the three next pieces properly, you lose your fear of “speed” as such even in the 1200s, and start to feel that it’s merely a stage where pieces “stick easily.” As the stretches of time in which you feel that way gradually get longer, the clear is just around the corner.
