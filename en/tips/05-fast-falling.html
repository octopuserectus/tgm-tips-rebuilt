The high-gravity stages at a glance

In the 300s, gravity speeds up from 2G to 4G; in the 400s, it slows down from 5G to 3G.
G was explained in Chapter 1. A piece appears, unrotated, one frame’s worth of falling below the spot where it would touch the top of the playfield, so the paths a piece can take at 2G through 5G look like this.

Of course, the figures above assume DAS was already charged before the piece appeared.
If you charge late, or gravity is higher than you expected, you end up with the piece snagging on the stack and not getting there.
And at 20G, this happens.
You can charge DAS before a piece appears, but there is no such thing as buffering the sideways movement itself. On its first frame a piece always appears straight below its spawn point.
The figure on the left is a famous way of getting stuck with the T, and once you are in it there is no way out. Rotate the T and you end up with three columns sitting over the hole, which only makes matters worse.
So when gravity is high, keep the middle of the stack high and use the lock delay to slide pieces along the surface.
That way, however fast pieces fall, you can take them anywhere you like.

How to actually stack this way is covered from the next chapter on.

Lock delay reset

Once you start sliding pieces over the stack, there is one thing you must keep in mind: the lock delay is reset whenever the piece drops down a step.
(When you stand an I piece upright its bottom ends up two rows lower, but that does not count as falling.)
Say the lock delay is 0.5 seconds, and 0.4 seconds after landing you shift the piece sideways so that it falls: from the new landing it takes another full 0.5 seconds to lock. You can do this as many times as you can make the piece fall, which buys you time before it actually locks when gravity is high.
What you have to watch out for is the pre-lock input: you assume the piece has locked and start charging DAS for the next one, but it has not locked yet and you end up moving the previous piece.
Ultimately you can prevent this by forcing the lock with a manual lock, but until you are used to it, the way to cope is to charge DAS a little late. The DAS delay is set quite a bit shorter than the lock delay, so even if you start charging the moment the piece appears you will still make it.
