Why build up the fifth column?

Let’s take a closer look at the idea from the previous chapter of keeping the middle high.

The T can be taken either left or right as long as column 5 is the highest.
The Z and the O can be taken either way when column 5 or column 6 is high.
You could call them pieces that go to the right easily.
The S can be taken either way when column 4 or column 5 is high.
You could call it a piece that goes to the left easily.
The J and the L can be taken either way if any one of columns 4, 5 and 6 is high.
Sometimes that takes IRS, but basically these two are the pieces with a wide range of movement.
The I can be taken either way if any one of columns 4 through 7 is high, but with this piece the question is more often whether you can stand it upright once you have got it there.

In other words, if column 5 is high, every piece can be taken either left or right. This is the first step toward conquering 20G.
But you can hardly go counting “column one, column two…” from the left in the middle of a game. Since pieces always spawn in the same place, what you should do instead is memorize “what to do to a freshly spawned piece in order to make column 5 high.”

Placements that build up the fifth column

“Standing an L-shaped piece (J or L) upright on the spot”
As the game speeds up you can’t help wanting to move pieces sideways, and the key is whether this placement occurs to you in mid-game and you can actually carry it out.
This goes for 20G beginners, of course, but also once you take up time attack, or when the pace at which pieces appear picks up in the advanced stages of TAP: fear makes you flatten the stack, and that tends to be where it starts falling apart. At times like that, remember this placement. You should find you can hold the stack together far better.
“Dropping a Z-shaped piece (Z or S) down the stairs”
With the Z and the S, the rotation system may be hard to memorize at first.
Just remember it as “drop it down the staircase by exactly one step.”
When you flip a J or an L over, it is the same as above: “shift it one column outward.”
“Standing the I in column 5”
Stood upright on the spot, the I makes column 6 high, so it needs a “left 1” (one tap to the left).
The figure on the right is a variation in which you move sideways first and then rotate.
The T and the O make column 5 high if you just drop them on the spot.
For the O, it is worth learning the “left 1” input as well; it comes in handy in all sorts of ways.
