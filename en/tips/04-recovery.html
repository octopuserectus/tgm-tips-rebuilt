Don’t stack pieces on top of holes

Recovering means clearing every line of the stack that forms the lid over the hole.
So the thinner you keep that lid, the easier the recovery.
It would defeat the purpose if you went to such lengths to avoid the space above a hole that your stack became a complete mess, but simply keeping in mind that you don’t stack on top of holes for no reason should make your recoveries a good deal more efficient.
→ →
Standing a piece upright means it takes up less width.
That helps you stay off the space above a hole, so keep it in mind as a principle.
→ →
As mentioned in Chapter 2, you often stand Z and S pieces upright and stack them on one another, but do that here and the lid never goes away, whereas a T does the job: a pattern that comes up fairly often.

Keep the top flat by using pieces laid flat

A misplaced I or the like sometimes leaves you with a piece of stack that juts out like a roof.
If you are so keen to clear the line that you jam a T or an S in upright, you will be left with a nasty shape.
The point is to use a J laid flat. That way the top is flat, so you can skim the whole thing away cleanly.

You want the idea of putting the J in first to occur to you even before the left side has been filled in.
